## 1. Niche and shopper

- Niche (and sub-niche): fashion-and-beauty lifestyle commerce for mid-premium lifestyle brands — clothing, accessories, and beauty sold as complete looks and routines; sub-niche is the style + skincare "mood" shopper, with mobile commerce as the primary battleground (per the merchant brief: "lifestyle brands — fashion/beauty, mobile commerce").
- Primary shopper: Maya, 31 — mobile-first; discovers fashion and beauty through social feeds and editorial and expects the storefront to feel like an extension of that content; technically comfortable (Shop Pay, one-tap reorders), short attention per screen, browses on her phone during commutes and buys when a look clicks; budgets $50-200 per order but buys repeatedly.
- Decision-maker: the shopper herself; no shared veto. The gift buyer (below) decides alone too.
- Gift buyer: secondary but real in this niche (~15-20% of orders); buys a set, kit, or gift-card when the recipient's taste or skin concern is known; wants presentation (gift wrap, note) and zero-risk returns on the recipient's behalf.
- How they decide: aspiration-led discovery, trust-led validation, impulse enabled by styled context. First purchases are researched (2-4 sessions: fit/fabric or ingredient/reviews); repeat purchases are fast and routine-based. Price is secondary to fit confidence, ingredient transparency, and brand identity — price-led discounting signals lower quality at this price point.
- Evidence: merchant brief (pipeline-state: niche, mode, rebrand context); feature inventory concepts available for the rebuild (lookbook, quick add, quick view, sticky add-to-cart, cart drawer, bundle builder, recently viewed, size chart, swatches, predictive search, product recommendations, complementary products, gift wrapping — all keep_concept: true in docs/feature-inventory.md); compliance constraints (no deceptive urgency/fictitious countdowns, 44x44 px touch targets, reviews only via app block, `<shopify-account>` required in header — docs/compliance-matrix.md); competitor inspection 2026-09-09 — Glossier (glossier.com: Shopify storefront, collections/products/search structure, routine-and-kit merchandising, mobile-first DTC beauty) and Reformation (thereformation.com: story-led home page leading with lifestyle positioning, editorial fashion) inspected via storefront reads; Sézane and Aesop read attempts blocked (403) — cited below as market references only, not inspected evidence.
- Assumptions (flagged, not facts): no merchant analytics or catalog were provided. Assume a mixed fashion + beauty catalog (clothing, accessories, skincare/beauty) of roughly 200-2,000 SKUs across ~10-20 collections; typical item price $28-300, beauty refills $18-90; 60-75% of sessions on mobile; drop-offs at unfiltered collection grids, fit/ingredient uncertainty on the PDP, and partial look/routine carts (to be validated by theme-qa-reviewer's QA pass). Assume merchants populate fit/fabric and ingredient metafields — the theme ships the blocks plus demo-store content; merchants who leave metafields empty degrade gracefully (blocks hide, never break). Assume a working Shopify customer-account setup (the account component is a Theme Store requirement).

## 2. Journey archetype

- Archetype: derived hybrid (not one of the four verbatim; derived per references/shopping-models.md method).
- Chain: **Moment -> Look -> Product -> Complete it**
- Derivation: Fashion is `Story -> Look -> Product -> Complete the look`; Beauty is `Concern -> Routine -> Ingredient -> Product`. A lifestyle fashion/beauty brand sells both verticals, and at this price point they share one decision DNA. The two entry hops collapse into a single shopper hop: the **Moment** — an occasion, mood, trend, or skin goal ("wedding guest", "weekend", "glow", "calm my redness"). That is the shopper's vocabulary; the merchant's categories are not. The **Look** is either a styled outfit (fashion) or a curated routine (beauty). The **Product** is the item inside the look. **Complete it** is complete-the-look and complete-the-routine: the natural last hop that raises order value without discounts. The trust hop is shared too: fashion validates with fit and fabric, beauty with ingredients and clinical claims; both land on the PDP as the same "will this work for me" block. Repeat purchase is shared: beauty restocks a routine, fashion restocks a favourite or answers the next occasion.
- Why this archetype fits: browsing IS part of the purchase in this niche — shoppers buy identity and mood, not specifications; impulse is enabled by styled imagery, not spec tables; trust is won by authenticity (fit, ingredients, real reviews), not by price; and completion logic (outfit/routine) is the merchant's natural AOV lever. Electronics and furniture archetypes (compare/config/room-scale) do not fit: there is no multi-model comparison ritual at this price point and no spatial purchase.

## 3. The seven shopping-model decisions

1. **Navigation model: moment-led primary navigation over a category spine.** Top level leads with shopper vocabulary — The Edit / occasions & moods / skin goals ("Glow", "Calm") — layered on classic entries (New, Clothing, Accessories, Beauty, Bestsellers) so repeat buyers get fast taxonomy paths. The mega menu blends moments and categories; mobile drawer mirrors the same order. Driven by: the archetype's first hop is the Moment — Maya arrives wanting to belong to a look or solve a skin goal, not to find a "category" (merchant vocabulary). Competitor: Reformation leads with story, not product type. Unusual for generic e-commerce: navigation is by moment/concern first, category second.

2. **Collection discovery: three entry types, self-narrowing grids.** (a) Moment/editorial landing pages — shop-the-look, occasion edits, concern edits — are the discovery hubs; (b) category collections serve quick and repeat buyers; (c) capsule/trend collections capture seasonality. Every collection grid carries storefront filters in the shopper's vocabulary: size, color, product type, price, availability, and — for beauty — concern/ingredient metafield filters, plus a "not sure? start with an edit" escape hatch. Driven by: fashion/beauty shoppers browse without a SKU in mind and abandon when a grid cannot narrow by their own words. Platform: storefront filtering supports variant options, price, availability, and metafield filters (verified, see §7); Theme Store requires filtering on collection and search pages.

3. **Search model: grouped predictive search that speaks the archetype's middle hops.** Shoppers type product types, moments/trends, and ingredients/concerns ("slip dress", "wedding guest", "linen", "niacinamide", "redness"). Predictive search must return grouped results — Looks & Guides (articles), Products, Collections — with styled product entries, and full search results must keep the same groups plus filters. Driven by: search vocabulary is the middle of the archetype chain; dead-end search is a known top drop-off in fashion/beauty, and bare-thumbnail results fail the styled-context need. Platform: predictive search supports product, collection, query, page, and article suggestions with typo tolerance (verified, see §7).

4. **PDP information architecture: impulse first, validation second, risk removal last.** Above the fold: styled gallery, name, price, size/variant picker, add-to-cart, and the "seen in / part of" look context (which look or routine this product completes). Below, in order: how-to-wear / what-it-does story; validation block — fit notes + model measurements + fabric for fashion, or key ingredients with one-line purpose + ingredient glossary links for beauty; size guide / ingredient details; care; evidence (photo reviews via the app block, rating); delivery and returns last. Driven by: the impulse decision happens in the hero; the trust decision happens immediately under it; delivery promises never outrank the hero claim (archetype order: Product validation before commitment). Platform: metafields and metaobject dynamic sources power the fit/ingredient blocks (verified, see §7).

5. **Cross-selling logic: complete-the-moment, never "related products".** On the PDP: the other items of the look/routine this product appears in (complementary block), then same-collection or same-concern alternatives. On look/routine pages: add-the-whole-look in one action. On cart: exactly one contextual completion prompt. For beauty repeaters: refill/reorder with a single dismissible subscription offer via selling plans. Driven by: the archetype's last hop — fashion shoppers complete looks, beauty shoppers complete routines; completeness, not discounting or urgency, is the AOV lever (compliance also bans fictitious countdowns and fake stock, so urgency-based cross-sell is off the table by rule).

6. **Cart continuation: drawer-first, reassuring, journey-connected.** Adding to cart opens the cart drawer immediately (mobile-first). It shows line items with per-item context (size/fit note for fashion, routine-step indicator for beauty), a "2 of 3 in your look/routine" completion state with the one contextual upsell, free-shipping progress, delivery window, free-returns reassurance, and a sticky checkout action — plus an explicit path back to the look/routine being completed. No fake timers, no fake stock claims. Driven by: partial look/routine carts are where sessions die between add-to-cart and checkout; trust-led buyers need returns and delivery certainty at the moment of commit. Platform: cart drawer state, free-shipping progress, and gift-wrap line properties are theme-side via the Cart Ajax API (verified, see §7).

7. **Mobile shopping flow: thumb-reach, tap-counted, separately designed.** Thumb-reach: sticky bottom add-to-cart on the PDP; quick-add from grids (add-to-cart in at most 2 taps from the grid, 3 from the PDP); cart drawer reachable in 1 tap from every screen; search reachable from every screen; all primary controls ≥44x44 px. Collapsible accordions below the PDP fold so the hero stays short. Collection filters open in a drawer/modal (per Shopify's filtering UX guidance) instead of a persistent column. Full-bleed imagery and shoppable look hotspots that do not lose the story when tapping a product. Desktop differs: persistent filter column, hover mega menu, look context visible beside the product without navigation loss. Driven by: 60-75% of this niche's sessions are mobile and browsing is part of the purchase — every extra tap or unreachable control ends the mood. Platform: quick-add and drawer behavior ride on the Cart Ajax API and storefront filtering (verified, see §7).

## 4. What the theme must make effortless

- Going from a moment (occasion, mood, skin goal) to a styled look or routine in two taps, without knowing the merchant's taxonomy (Journeys 1-2).
- Validating fit (size guide, fit notes, model measurements) or ingredients (inline glossary) without leaving the PDP (Journeys 1-2).
- Completing the look or routine — from the PDP, from look/routine pages, and from the cart — in one action (Journeys 1-2, 4).
- Reordering a known routine or favourite in under a minute from the customer account, with a single dismissible subscription offer (Journey 3).
- Trust at the last step: delivery window and free returns visible at the moment of commit (all journeys).

## 5. Why this shopping model fits the niche

Moment-led navigation matches how fashion/beauty lifestyle shoppers actually enter a storefront — from a story, an occasion, or a skin goal — instead of forcing them to translate their desire into merchant categories. The Look hop makes merchandising concrete: styled outfits and curated routines convert browsing into cart because the shopper sees the complete result, not a lone SKU. The Product hop carries the trust layer this niche demands — fit/fabric or ingredients with evidence — which is where first-time buyers previously hesitated. Complete-it converts the archetype's natural last step into order value without discounting, and the repeat hop (restock/reorder) retains customers the niche depends on. Every decision derives from who buys (mobile-first, trust-led, aspiration-led), how they decide (researched first, routine repeat), and the platform's verified capabilities — not from generic e-commerce practice.

## 6. Drop-off risks and design responses

| Risk (where sessions die today) | Design response |
|---|---|
| Unfiltered collection grids lose browsing shoppers | Filters in the shopper's vocabulary (size, color, price, availability, concern/ingredient) plus "start with an edit" escape hatch (decisions 2, 7) |
| Fit uncertainty kills fashion PDP-to-cart | Fit notes, model measurements, size guide, fabric block, free returns visible before commit (decisions 4, 6) |
| Ingredient opacity kills beauty PDP-to-cart | Key-ingredient purpose near the hero, inline glossary, evidence block with photo reviews (decisions 3, 4) |
| Partial look/routine carts abandoned | Cart completion state ("2 of 3"), one contextual upsell, free-shipping progress, returns + delivery window (decision 6) |
| Dead-end search ends the mood | Grouped predictive results incl. Looks & Guides; styled product entries; filters on results (decisions 3, 7) |
| Repeaters re-research every restock | My Routine / favourites in the account sheet; one-tap reorder; single dismissible subscription offer (decision 6, Journey 3) |

## 7. Platform capability notes

Capabilities assumed below were fetched and verified 2026-09-09 from shopify.dev; mark "verify current" before execution, never treat as permanent.

- Storefront filtering: filters on availability, category, price, tags, product type, vendor, variant options (size/color), and product/variant metafields; AND across filter groups, OR within; max 25 filters; filters reflected in URL params. Verified at https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/navigation-search/filtering/storefront-filtering (implementation: https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/navigation-search/filtering/storefront-filtering/support-storefront-filtering; UX: https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/navigation-search/filtering/storefront-filtering/storefront-filtering-ux). Theme Store requires filtering on collection and search pages — https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/store/requirements.
- Predictive search: suggestions for products, collections, queries, pages, articles with typo tolerance; searchable properties include title, product_type, variants.title, vendor; grouped section-render pattern. Verified at https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/navigation-search/search/predictive-search and https://shopify.dev/docs/api/ajax/reference/predictive-search; UX guidelines at https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/navigation-search/search/predictive-search-ux.
- Product/collection metafields and metaobjects: accessed in Liquid via `product.metafields`/`collection.metafields`; connectable to section/block settings as dynamic sources in the theme editor (powers fit/fabric and ingredient blocks). Verified at https://shopify.dev/docs/api/liquid/objects/metafields and https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/architecture/settings/dynamic-sources.
- Cart behavior: Cart Ajax API — add one or multiple variants in one call, line-item properties (gift wrap), cart notes/attributes, selling plans (subscriptions), shipping-rate generation; drawer/upsell presentation is theme-side. Verified at https://shopify.dev/docs/api/ajax/reference/cart.
- Customer-account-aware sections: `<shopify-account>` component in the header (required on desktop and mobile for Theme Store submissions) with account sheet and menu links; legacy customer accounts deprecated; My Routine lives in the customer-account menu/templates. Verified at https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/customer-engagement/account-component and https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/sign-in; requirement at https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/store/requirements.
- Reviews: theme ships the `@app` review block slot; review rendering is the merchant's app's job (app-like functionality must not be baked into the theme — docs/compliance-matrix.md, docs/feature-inventory.md). Verify current app-block placement at https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/store/requirements.
